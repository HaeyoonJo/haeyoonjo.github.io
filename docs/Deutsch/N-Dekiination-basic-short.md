## 6. N-Deklination (Weak Noun Declension)

### 6.1 Basic Principle

N-Deklination is a special declension pattern for certain masculine nouns.

These nouns usually receive **-n** or **-en** in all cases except Nominativ Singular.

### 6.2 Example: der Junge

| Kasus | Form |
|---|---|
| Nominativ | der Junge |
| Akkusativ | den Jungen |
| Dativ | dem Jungen |
| Genitiv | des Jungen |

### 6.3 Common Examples

| Nominativ | Akkusativ / Dativ / Genitiv |
|---|---|
| der Junge | den / dem / des Jungen |
| der Student | den / dem / des Studenten |
| der Kollege | den / dem / des Kollegen |
| der Mensch | den / dem / des Menschen |
| der Herr | den / dem / des Herrn |

### 6.4 Important Points to Remember

- N-Deklination mainly applies to masculine nouns.
- The noun usually receives **-n** or **-en** in Akkusativ, Dativ, and Genitiv Singular.
- The article also changes according to the Kasus.
- Learn the common nouns together with their declension forms.

**Example:**

- `Der Student lernt Deutsch.` – The student learns German.
- `Ich sehe den Studenten.` – I see the student.
- `Ich helfe dem Studenten.` – I help the student.
